The Moon and the Saga

How a rules engine resolves one of Magic's strangest interactions — Blood Moon meets Urza's Saga — told twice: once for the table, once for the engineers.

The verdict up front: under Blood Moon, Urza's Saga becomes a Mountain that taps for {R}, loses all three chapters, and — this is the strange part — is never sacrificed. It just sits there. Forever.

A simplified model of the engine

The engine never trusts what is printed on a card. Every time it needs to know what a permanent is — its types, its abilities, its power — it starts from the printed card and pushes it through a pipeline of layers: first effects that change what the card is (its types), then effects that change what it does (its abilities). What comes out the far end is the only version of the card the game ever sees.

Urza's Saga is a Saga — an enchantment that tells a story in chapters. It collects a lore counter each turn, each counter unlocks a chapter ability, and when the story is finished (counters ≥ final chapter), the Saga is sacrificed. Blood Moon says seven small words: “Nonbasic lands are Mountains.” Watch what those words do to the story.

I

The Moon changes what the Saga is

Blood Moon acts in the type-changing layer. It replaces the Saga's land subtypes with one word: Mountain. But here is the first subtlety — “Saga” is not a land subtype. It is an enchantment subtype, so the Moon cannot touch it. The permanent comes out of the pipeline as an Enchantment Land — Mountain Saga. Still a Saga. Still telling a story. In an early version of the engine, Blood Moon wrongly stripped card types too (an artifact land stopped being an artifact); the engine now replaces only the land subtypes, exactly as printed.

II

Becoming a Mountain has consequences

The rules say a land that becomes a Mountain loses its printed abilities and gains the one intrinsic ability every Mountain has: {T}: Add {R}. Nobody prints that text on a Mountain — the game just knows. The engine does the same: the moment the pipeline resolves the type change, it derives the red mana ability out of thin air and deletes the chapters. Our Saga now has zero chapter abilities.

III

Seven words keep it alive

Here is the heart of the whole interaction. The sacrifice rule for Sagas contains a quiet qualifier: it only applies to a Saga “with one or more chapter abilities.” Our Moon-struck Saga has zero. So the sacrifice check simply never fires — and neither does the rule that adds a lore counter each turn, because it carries the same qualifier.

Why do the rules bother with that clause? Because of an arithmetic trap. A Saga with no chapters has a final chapter number of 0 — and every Saga always has at least zero counters. Without the qualifier, counters ≥ final chapter would always be true, and a blanked Saga would be sacrificed the instant the game looked at it. The seven words flip the outcome from “dies immediately” to “lives forever.”

IV

And if the Moon sets…

One more subtlety the engine had to get right. The rule that gives a Saga its very first lore counter — the one it gets as it enters the battlefield — does not carry the seven-word qualifier. So a Saga played under Blood Moon still quietly takes that first counter, even though nothing else happens. If Blood Moon later leaves the battlefield, the chapters return, the counter is still there, and the story resumes at chapter II — it does not restart. The engine gets this right because it suppressed only what the rules say to suppress, not everything that looked suppressed.

What this looks like in a real game

Honesty corner: in the engine today, this exact pairing can't yet be assembled from a legal deck — Urza's Saga's first two chapters (“this Saga gains an ability”) are still on the card-authoring worklist, and the engine refuses to start a game containing any card it can't fully simulate. The machinery above is fully built and proven — using a stand-in: a Saga turned face down (via Reality Shift's manifest) also has zero chapter abilities, and the engine proves the whole no-counters / no-chapters / no-sacrifice behaviour on that pair in real games, both directions. The rules, verbatim

Everything below hangs on four rules. The engine's test suite cites these by number; the highlighted phrase in 714.4 is what the whole interaction turns on.

CR 714.4 If the number of lore counters on a Saga permanent with one or more chapter abilities is greater than or equal to its final chapter number, and it isn't the source of a chapter ability that has triggered but not yet left the stack, that Saga's controller sacrifices it. This state-based action doesn't use the stack.
CR 714.2d A Saga's final chapter number is the greatest value among chapter abilities it has. If a Saga somehow has no chapter abilities, its final chapter number is 0.
CR 714.3a / 714.3b As a Saga … enters the battlefield, its controller puts a lore counter on it.  /  As a player's precombat main phase begins, that player puts a lore counter on each Saga they control with one or more chapter abilities.
CR 708.2a If a face-up permanent is turned face down … it becomes a 2/2 face-down creature with no text, no name, no subtypes, and no mana cost.

The seven words in 714.4 are an exemption clause, and 714.2d is why it must exist: lore ≥ 0 is always true, so without the clause a blanked Saga would be sacrificed instantly by the state-based-action loop. With it, the check never applies. Note two asymmetries that matter later: 714.3b (the per-turn counter) carries the clause, but 714.3a (the ETB counter) does not; and 708.2a removes subtypes, which a Layer-6 ability-removal never does (CR 613.1f removes abilities, not subtypes).

Three batches, one interaction

The engine work landed across three primitive batches. Each fixed a different link:

batchrulewhat changed
PB-DX27CR 613.1d LayerModification::SetLandTypes — Blood Moon replaces only land subtypes. Before this, it stripped the Artifact card type from artifact lands. The Saga subtype survives because ALL_LAND_TYPES contains "Urza's" but not "Saga" (CR 205.3h: Saga is an enchantment subtype).
PB-DX43CR 305.6 / 305.7 The intrinsic {T}: Add {R} is derived at the end of the layer-4 walk for any object with the land type and a basic land subtype — no printed text needed. CR 305.7's ability-loss was folded into SetLandTypes itself, because leaving it as Blood Moon's own Layer-6 removal would have wiped the derived mana ability too and broken Blood Moon entirely.
PB-DX49CR 714 Every CR 714 decision now reads the layer-resolved card instead of the printed one, through a single query. This is the exemption clause becoming real.

The bug that was

Before PB-DX49, all five CR 714 decision sites read the printed definition. Printed, Urza's Saga always has three chapters — so the engine would have kept adding lore counters, kept firing chapter triggers, and sacrificed the Saga anyway. Blood Moon would have killed the Saga instead of preserving it: the exact inverse of the famous interaction, wrong at every site, and wrong at rest (no later event self-heals it).

The fix: one question, asked once

The shipped shape is a single read-only query, rules::saga::saga_view, that answers every CR 714 question — is this still a Saga? which chapter abilities does it retain after layer resolution? what is its final chapter? — consumed by all five behavioural sites. Under it sits one ability-blanking predicate, layers::abilities_are_blanked, which covers both ways a permanent can lose its abilities, and delegates classification to an exhaustive, no-wildcard match over every layer-modification variant — so if a future batch adds a third blanking channel, the compiler refuses to build until it is classified. After code review, “exactly one such predicate exists in the tree” is machine-enforced, not just asserted.

Two blanking channels, one deliberate difference

Face-down is not a side note — it is the second, independent door into “a Saga with no chapter abilities,” and CR 714.4 doesn't care which door was used. Reality Shift makes its victim's controller manifest the top card of their library; if that card is a Saga, it enters as an anonymous 2/2 with — per 708.2a — no abilities and no subtypes. The two channels agree on almost everything and differ on exactly one measured point:

That single differing row is load-bearing. The original defect filing treated the surviving ETB counter as part of the bug; reading the rule showed it is correct — and it matters, because if Blood Moon later leaves, the Saga still holds that counter and resumes at chapter II rather than restarting at I. One query, two fields (“is it a Saga” vs “does it retain chapters”), and each site asks the field its rule actually reads. The engine had this inconsistency half-built already: the ETB-trigger queue checked face-down while the Saga sites didn't — the same subsystem, disagreeing with itself.

Why the Moon carries the blanking, not a Layer-6 removal

An engineering wrinkle worth savouring: Blood Moon's card def carries essentially one static — SetLandTypes(Mountain) — and the engine derives both the ability loss (CR 305.7) and the {T}: Add {R} grant (CR 305.6) from the type change itself. The obvious alternative, giving Blood Moon its own Layer-6 RemoveAllAbilities, fails twice: it would timestamp-race other Layer-6 grants (Chromatic Lantern's, The World Tree's — CR 305.7's last sentence forbids removing abilities granted by other effects), and it would delete the engine's own derived mana ability, breaking Blood Moon outright. A grep-shaped moral came out of this in testing: after the change, the two moons blank abilities through a variant no RemoveAllAbilities string-search can see — the blanker census that used a grep agreed with reality by coincidence of totals, not membership. The census now calls the classifier instead of grepping for a name.

The last row deserves its footnote. urzas_saga.rs exists, but chapters I and II are literal GainLife(0) placeholders behind TODOs, so the deck validator — which refuses any card the engine can't fully simulate (an architecture invariant: no mid-game surprises, ever) — rejects it. The corner-case audit therefore marks this interaction PARTIAL: engine half covered, card half gated on authoring the two “this Saga gains an ability” chapters. Notably, the primitives for that already exist with corpus users (AddManaAbility / AddActivatedAbility + EffectFilter::Source) — an earlier note claiming a missing “gained-ability tracking” primitive was checked and found wrong. What remains is ordinary card work, not engine work.

Cost of the design, disclosed rather than buried: routing every SBA pass through the layer-aware query costs a real ~1.7% on the state-based-action microbench — measured against the merge base in an isolated worktree after a first “no regression” claim from a branch-only run was refuted in review. The residual is inherent to asking one honest question instead of five cheap wrong ones.
